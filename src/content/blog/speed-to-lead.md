Picture a homeowner on a Tuesday night, laptop open, looking for someone to fix a leaking hot water system. They fill in the form on three websites, one after the other. The first business replies in four minutes with a friendly text and a link to book. The second calls the next morning. The third sends an email two days later.

Who gets the job? Almost always the first one. Not because they're cheaper or better, but because they showed up while the customer still cared.

That is **speed to lead**: the time between a new enquiry arriving and your first real reply. For most small businesses it's the cheapest growth lever they aren't pulling.

## Why the first reply matters so much

When someone sends an enquiry, they're at peak interest. They have the problem in front of them and they've set aside a moment to deal with it. Every minute after that, their attention drifts back to work, kids, dinner, or the next search result.

A fast reply does three things at once:

- **It catches them while they're still looking.** You're part of the decision before your competitors are.
- **It signals how you'll treat them.** A business that answers quickly feels organised and reliable.
- **It stops the shopping around.** Once someone has a booking or a quote in hand, most stop filling in forms.

Slow replies don't just lose the lead. They often hand it to a competitor you were spending the same ad budget against.

## Where enquiries go to die

Most businesses don't ignore leads on purpose. Leads slip through the cracks in the same few places:

1. **After hours and weekends.** A large share of enquiries arrive when the office is closed. By Monday, the customer has moved on.
2. **The shared inbox.** Website forms land in an inbox that three people can see and nobody owns.
3. **Busy hands.** Tradies on site, dentists with a patient in the chair, agents at an open home. The phone rings out and the missed call never gets returned.
4. **Too many channels.** Facebook messages, Instagram DMs, Google Business chats, web forms and texts, all in different apps.

If you're not sure how quick you are, try a simple test: send an enquiry through your own website on a Friday afternoon and time how long it takes for a real reply.

## How to reply in minutes without hiring more staff

You don't need a receptionist on call around the clock. You need a system that does the first step for you, then hands the conversation to a person.

### 1. Send an instant, human-sounding first reply

The moment a form is submitted, send a short text and email in your own voice. Thank them, confirm what they asked for, and give them a clear next step, such as a booking link or a time you'll call. Keep it to two or three sentences. It should read like a message from a person, not a ticket number.

### 2. Bring every channel into one inbox

Route web forms, social messages, Google chats and texts into one place, so nothing sits unseen in an app nobody checked. One inbox also means one history: whoever picks up the conversation can see everything already said.

### 3. Let an AI assistant handle the first questions

A well-trained chatbot can answer the common questions (prices, service areas, opening hours, what to bring) and book the appointment straight into your calendar, at 11pm on a Sunday. When a question needs a human, it should say so and pass the conversation on with full context.

### 4. Turn missed calls into conversations

When you can't pick up, an automatic text can go out within seconds: *"Sorry we missed your call, we're with a customer. How can we help?"* Many people would rather reply to a text than leave a voicemail anyway.

### 5. Follow up until you get an answer

Plenty of leads go quiet after the first reply, not because they've lost interest but because life got in the way. A gentle sequence of two or three follow-ups over a week wins back a surprising number of them. Stop the sequence automatically as soon as they reply or book.

## What to measure

Pick a few numbers and check them monthly:

- **Average time to first reply**, split into business hours and after hours.
- **Enquiry-to-booking rate**: of all the leads that came in, how many became an appointment or a quote.
- **Missed calls returned**, and how quickly.

If your first-reply time drops from hours to minutes, the booking rate usually follows. You'll have spent nothing extra on ads; you'll simply be keeping more of the leads you already paid for.

## The takeaway

Customers rarely choose the best business. They choose the best business *that got back to them*. Make sure that's you, at any hour, without asking your team to live on their phones.
